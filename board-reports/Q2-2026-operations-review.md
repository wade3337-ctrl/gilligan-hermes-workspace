# Q2 2026 — Operations Review
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors, quarterly meeting
**Prepared by:** Jason Wade, COO
**Date:** July 2026 · **Period covered:** Q2 2026 (April–June), with H1 context

> **Basis of preparation.** All revenue figures in this report are taken from the **Controller's financial
> statements** (the same income statement presented to this Board). Production and crew-hour figures come
> from our operating system, which is the only source for them — no equivalent exists in the financial
> statements. Sections are labelled accordingly.

---

## The quarter in one line

**Revenue grew for a second consecutive quarter, reversing a four-quarter decline, and crew productivity
reached its highest level on record — $134.80 per crew-hour against a $130 target.**

The first half finished at **91.6% of plan**. That shortfall was created in Q1 and largely recovered in Q2.
**The remaining gap to the annual goal is a capacity question, not a productivity one.**

## 2. Productivity — the core operating metric *(operating system)*

We manage crew productivity as **revenue per crew-hour**. The 2026 target is **$130**.

| Quarter | Revenue produced | Crew hours | Per crew-hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2025 | $5.23M | 43,398 | $120.42 |
| Q2 2025 | $5.11M | 41,218 | $124.04 |
| Q3 2025 | $5.63M | 45,841 | $122.89 |
| Q4 2025 | $5.36M | 40,968 | $130.79 |
| Q1 2026 | $5.09M | 40,601 | $125.42 |
| **Q2 2026** | **$5.66M** | **41,964** | **$134.80** |

**Q2 2026 is the highest productivity quarter in the series, and the first to clear target with margin.**

The most important line in this report:

> **In Q2 we produced 10.6% more revenue on 1.8% more crew hours.**
> Essentially all of the gain came from productivity, not from added labor.

For the half year, productivity averaged **$130.19 per crew-hour against a $130 target — on plan to the
penny — while total crew hours ran 2.4% *below* H1 2025.**

**Conclusion: the operating engine is performing to specification. We are not losing ground on efficiency,
pricing discipline or crew execution. We are constrained on the volume of hours worked.**

## 3. Position against the annual goal

The 2026 revenue plan is **$25.1M**. Through June we have delivered **$11.22M**.

**The second half requires $2.31M per month, July through December.**

What that means operationally:

| Monthly revenue | Crew hours required at target productivity | vs. H1 monthly average |
|---|---|---|
| $2.20M | 16,900 | +23% |
| **$2.31M — required** | **17,800** | **+29%** |

- H1 averaged **13,761 crew hours per month**.
- Our strongest month, April, delivered **16,513 hours**.
- **The required run-rate is roughly 8% above our best month, sustained for five consecutive months.**

**Two observations I want the Board to have plainly:**

1. **The current H2 plan of $2.2M/month does not reach the goal.** It was built assuming H1 would deliver
   $12.25M. H1 delivered $11.22M, and the plan was never re-based. Executed exactly as written it lands at
   approximately **$24.3M — about $800K short.**

2. **The gap cannot be closed through productivity alone.** If crew hours stay at the H1 average, the
   required productivity becomes **$173.81 per crew-hour** — a 34% increase over a target we are already
   meeting. That is not a realistic operating assumption and I will not present it as one.

**The path to $25.1M therefore runs through capacity — hours available to sell and to work — or through
price. Most likely both.** Every dollar of productivity above $130 reduces the hours we must add.

## 4. What is already committed

Of the $25.1M goal, approximately **$20.7M is covered** by revenue recognized to date, contracted municipal
work, sold work already scheduled, and risk-adjusted pipeline.

**The uncovered balance is roughly $4.4M of genuinely new business** that must be sold and produced in the
remainder of the year. There is no unrecognized backlog behind this figure — work already sold but dated
into 2027 has been deliberately excluded rather than counted toward this year.

*(Coverage analysis is drawn from the operating system, not the financial statements.)*

## 5. Systems and operating discipline

- **Executive reporting suite deployed.** Six management dashboards — revenue performance, production
  performance, crew performance, sales pipeline, the weekly sales-and-production meeting, and municipal
  budget tracking — now run on live data with a single shared definition of each metric, replacing
  manually maintained spreadsheets.
- **Municipal budget forecasting is now automated** from contract data rather than a hand-built workbook,
  removing a recurring source of reconciliation error.
- **Access to management reporting is permission-controlled** and administered without developer involvement.
- **Reconciliation with the Controller is ongoing and holding.** Our operating system's revenue reporting
  agreed with the financial statements to within **1.3% for the first half**, and matched full-year 2025 to
  within rounding. Individual quarters diverge by up to 3% on billing timing, which is expected. Management
  reporting and financial reporting tell the same story.

## 6. Risks and watch items

| Item | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Capacity to deliver the H2 run-rate** | The primary risk. Requires ~29% more crew hours per month than H1 averaged. Needs a staffing and scheduling answer before Q4. |
| **H2 plan not re-based after Q1** | The published monthly plan reaches ~$24.3M, not $25.1M. Recommend formally re-basing so the organization works to a number that reaches the goal. |
| **Pipeline conversion** | Roughly $4.4M of the goal depends on business not yet sold. |
| **Seasonality** | Q4 is historically our softest quarter (Q4 2025: $5.02M). The plan places $2.05–2.20M per month into that window. |

---

## 7. What I would ask of the Board

1. **Endorse re-basing the H2 plan** to a monthly figure that actually reaches $25.1M.
2. **Direction on capacity.** Closing the gap requires materially more crew hours than we ran in H1. That is
   a hiring, retention and scheduling decision with cost implications, and I would like alignment before
   committing to it.
3. **Acknowledge the productivity result.** Crews delivered target productivity through a soft first quarter
   and exceeded it in the second, on fewer hours than last year. Whatever we decide on volume, the operating
   discipline is working.

### Notes on the figures

- **Revenue** — Controller's income statement ("Total Income"), the same basis presented in the financial
  package. Full-year reference points: 2023 $22.56M · 2024 $22.63M · 2025 $21.32M.
- **Production, crew hours and revenue per crew-hour** — operating system. There is no equivalent in the
  financial statements; these measure work completed, not work invoiced, so they will not tie exactly to
  revenue in any single period.
- **Production counts work completed through the end of each period only.**
- **July is excluded from every actual figure.** The month is open and invoicing typically completes 3–10
  days after month end, so a partial July would understate results.
- Operating-system data as of July 28, 2026. Financial statements as of the June 2026 close.
