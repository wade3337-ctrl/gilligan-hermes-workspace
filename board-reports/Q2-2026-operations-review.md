# Q2 2026 — Operations Review
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors, quarterly meeting
**Prepared by:** Jason Wade, COO
**Date:** July 2026 · **Period covered:** Q2 2026 (April–June), with H1 context

---

## The quarter in one line

**Q2 2026 was our strongest quarter in at least six quarters — $6.07M in revenue — and crew productivity
reached its highest level on record at $134.80 per crew-hour, 3.7% above target.**

The first half finished at 91.6% of plan. That shortfall was created in Q1 and substantially recovered in
Q2. **The remaining gap to our annual goal is a capacity question, not a productivity one.**

## 2. Productivity — the core operating metric

We manage crew productivity as **revenue per crew-hour**. The 2026 target is **$130**.

| Quarter | Revenue produced | Crew hours | Per crew-hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2025 | $5.23M | 43,398 | $120.42 |
| Q2 2025 | $5.11M | 41,218 | $124.04 |
| Q3 2025 | $5.63M | 45,841 | $122.89 |
| Q4 2025 | $5.36M | 40,968 | $130.79 |
| Q1 2026 | $5.09M | 40,601 | $125.42 |
| **Q2 2026** | **$5.66M** | **41,964** | **$134.80** |

**Q2 2026 is the highest productivity quarter in the series, and the first to clear target with margin.**

The most important line in this report:

> **In Q2 we produced 10.6% more revenue on 1.8% more crew hours.**
> Essentially all of the gain came from productivity, not from added labor.

For the half year, **productivity averaged $130.19 per crew-hour against a $130 target — on plan to the
penny — while total crew hours were 2.4% *below* H1 2025.**

**Conclusion: the operating engine is performing to specification. We are not losing ground on efficiency,
pricing discipline, or crew execution. We are constrained on volume of hours worked.**

## 3. Position against the annual goal

The 2026 revenue plan is **$25.1M**. Through June we have delivered **$11.22M**.

**What the second half requires: $2.31M per month, July through December.**

For context on what that means operationally:

| Monthly revenue | Crew hours required (at target productivity) | vs. H1 monthly average |
|---|---|---|
| $2.20M | 16,900 | +23% |
| **$2.31M — required** | **17,800** | **+29%** |

- H1 averaged **13,761 crew hours per month**.
- Our strongest month of the year, April, delivered **16,513 hours**.
- **The required run-rate is roughly 8% above our best month, sustained for five consecutive months.**

**Two honest observations for the Board:**

1. **The current H2 plan of $2.2M/month does not reach the goal.** It was built assuming H1 would deliver
   $12.25M. H1 delivered $11.22M, and the plan was never re-based. Executed exactly as written, it lands at
   approximately **$24.3M — about $800K short.**

2. **The gap cannot be closed through productivity alone.** If crew hours remain at the H1 average, the
   required productivity would be **$173.81 per crew-hour** — a 34% increase over a target we are already
   meeting. That is not a realistic operating assumption, and I would not present it as one.

**Therefore the path to $25.1M runs through capacity — hours available to sell and to work — or through
price. Most likely a combination.** Every dollar of productivity above $130 reduces the hours we must add.

## 4. What is already committed

Of the $25.1M goal, **$20.7M is covered** by revenue recognized to date, contracted municipal work,
sold work already scheduled, and risk-adjusted pipeline.

**The uncovered balance is $4.4M of genuinely new business** that must be sold and produced in the
remainder of the year. There is no unrecognized backlog behind this figure — work already sold but dated
into 2027 has been deliberately excluded rather than counted toward this year.

---

## 5. Systems and operating discipline

- **Executive reporting suite deployed.** Six management dashboards — revenue performance, production
  performance, crew performance, sales pipeline, the weekly sales-and-production meeting, and municipal
  budget tracking — now run on live data with a single shared definition of each metric, replacing
  manually maintained spreadsheets.
- **Municipal budget forecasting is now automated** off contract data rather than a hand-built workbook,
  eliminating a recurring source of reconciliation error.
- **Access to management reporting is now permission-controlled** and administered without developer
  involvement.
- **Financial reconciliation with the Controller is ongoing.** Our operating system's revenue reporting
  now ties to the accounting books within **1.3%** for the first half, and matched full-year 2025 to
  within rounding. Management and financial reporting tell the same story.

---

## 6. Risks and watch items

| Item | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Capacity to deliver the H2 run-rate** | The primary risk. Requires ~29% more crew hours per month than H1 averaged. Needs a staffing and scheduling answer before Q4. |
| **H2 plan not re-based after Q1** | The published monthly plan reaches ~$24.3M, not $25.1M. Recommend formally re-basing so the organization is working to a number that reaches the goal. |
| **Pipeline conversion** | $4.4M of the goal depends on new business not yet sold. |
| **Seasonality** | Q4 is historically our softest quarter (Q4 2025: $5.14M). The plan places $2.05M–$2.20M per month into that window. |

---

## 7. What I would ask of the Board

1. **Endorse re-basing the H2 plan** to a monthly figure that actually reaches $25.1M.
2. **Direction on capacity.** Closing the gap requires materially more crew hours than we ran in H1.
   That is a hiring, retention and scheduling decision with cost implications, and I would like alignment
   before committing.
3. **Acknowledge the productivity result.** Crews delivered target productivity through a soft first
   quarter and exceeded it in the second. Whatever we decide on volume, the operating discipline is working.

- Revenue against plan is stated on the **accounting basis** used in the Controller's financial statements.
