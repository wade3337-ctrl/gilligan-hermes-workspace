# Q2 2026 — COO Operating Plan
**Jason Wade · Board of Directors, quarterly meeting**

> *Financial results are presented by the CFO. This covers operations: what the numbers tell us about
> how the business is running, and what I am doing about it.*

## Where operations stand

- **The crews are performing.** Productivity reached **$136.69 per crew-hour against a $130 target** in Q2 —
  our best on record. We produced **15.3% more than last year's Q2 on 4.6% more hours**.
- **The constraint is capacity, not execution.** We are not losing ground on efficiency or pricing. We do
  not have enough working hours to hit $25.1M.
- **The team is already stretched.** Field headcount is down about 8 people year over year, the remaining
  crew absorbed it with no drop in total hours, and they now average 8.9-hour days.

**One sentence: the engine is running well and it is running at its limit.**

---

## The three things I am doing about it

### 1. Field capacity — the binding constraint

**Problem:** the second half needs ~24% more crew hours per month than the first half delivered. That is
roughly **19 additional field staff** — about 76 work a typical weekday today, we need about 95.

**Plan:**
- Recruiting targeted at ~19 net additions, front-loaded — anyone hired after September cannot
  meaningfully contribute to Q4.
- Retention first: we lost ~8 field staff over the year and covered it with the crew we kept. Replacing
  attrition is not growth — recruiting has to outrun it before it adds a single net hour.
- Report net field headcount to this Board monthly, not revenue alone.

**How we will know it is working:** people working per weekday, tracked monthly against the ~95 needed.

### 2. Utilization — protect the gain we already made

**Problem:** this year's entire productivity improvement came from converting non-revenue time into
billable time. Non-revenue hours fell **29.3%** — from 22.8% of the day to 17.0%. That gain is fragile.

**Plan:**
- Hold the weekly sales-and-production meeting on live data — that discipline is what produced the shift,
  and it is what will keep it.
- Treat 17% as a floor, not an achievement. Every point recovered is worth ~205 crew-hours a month at no
  added cost; every point lost has to be bought back with hiring.

**How we will know it is working:** non-revenue share of hours, reported monthly.

### 3. Price and job mix — the lever we have not pulled

**Problem:** our on-the-job rate is **flat to slightly down (−0.8%)**. All of this year's gain came from
utilization; none came from price. That is the cheapest lever available and it is untouched.

**Plan:**
- Review pricing and job mix on the work we are winning, starting with the segments where rate has slipped.
- Every $5 of on-the-job rate removes roughly one crew's monthly output from the hiring requirement.

**How we will know it is working:** revenue per revenue-bearing hour, reported monthly.

---

## Two things I am correcting

- **The H2 plan does not reach the goal.** It was built assuming a first half of $12.25M; we delivered
  $11.22M and the plan was never re-based. As written it lands around **$24.3M**. I am re-basing it to the
  **$2.31M per month** the goal actually requires, so the organization is working to a real number.
- **Management reporting had a date defect** that placed completed work in the wrong month — in some views
  it hid entire crew-months. It is identified, fixed, and being deployed. Operational reporting now
  reconciles to the CFO's statements within 1.3% for the half.

---

## What I will bring back next quarter

- Net field headcount against the ~95 target.
- Non-revenue share of hours against the 17% floor.
- On-the-job rate against this quarter's baseline.
- Progress against the re-based $2.31M monthly plan.
