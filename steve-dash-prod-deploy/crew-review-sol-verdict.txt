
- Emptying the table while the new CFM remains deployed silently restores the attribution bug.
- Dropping the table before reverting every served CFM copy causes runtime errors.
- It destroys any prior production table contents.

Correct rollback order is: restore old CFM files in every served root, verify traffic is on the old version, then restore the backed-up DB rows/schema. Do not use an empty override table as a successful rollback.

### 6. Production-only failure modes

- The `GSTS` ColdFusion datasource is used for the queries; there is no separate `Workbench` datasource. The datasource’s SQL login must have cross-database `SELECT` permission on Workbench.
- The deployment operator separately needs `CREATE TABLE`, `CREATE INDEX`, `DELETE`, and `INSERT` rights in Workbench.
- Database ownership chaining does not automatically guarantee permission for these ad hoc three-part-name queries.
- A case-sensitive production database could expose object/name differences not seen in play.
- Existing `ix_por` is checked only by name and object, not by its columns or uniqueness. An incorrectly defined index with that name is accepted.
- Production blocking/load may be much higher. Hundreds of individual inserts plus a full delete increase the inconsistent-data window.
- `NOLOCK` on two override joins permits dirty/inconsistent attribution during deployment.
- The static 564-row map has no go-forward process for proposals reassigned after July 21; figures will drift again even after a successful release.
